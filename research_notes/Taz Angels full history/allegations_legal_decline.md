# Taz's Angels: Allegations, Legal Matters, Decline and Current Status (2011–2026)

Method note: The search tool worked, but direct page fetches were blocked by this session's network policy for hollywoodstreetking.com, miaminewtimes.com, sportskeeda.com and viraltalky.com. Every finding below comes from search-result snippets and summaries, not from full-text reading. Quotes and dates should be re-checked against the live pages before anything is relied on for legal clearance. Labels used: **VERIFIED** (court records, police/FBI/DOJ, credible mainstream outlet); **REPORTED** (a single blog or outlet, or tabloid/trade coverage with no primary documentation); **RUMOR/ALLEGATION** (forums, anonymous sourcing, AI summaries, social posts).

## 1. Origin and spread of the escort / "paid companionship" allegations

### Takeaway
The escort/prostitution allegations came from gossip blogs, urban-radio sites, forums and anonymous "former prospect" quotes, mostly from 2014 to 2016. They peaked in late January to early February 2016 with an unsourced claim that the FBI was investigating, attributed to Don Diva magazine. No police, court or mainstream-press source documents them. Former members (notably Demi Rose) and the group itself publicly denied being an escort operation.

### Cited Findings
- **REPORTED:** Taz's Angels was founded around 2011. It was known online for "seductive" pictures and nightlife appearances. XXL called them the "it" girls of Miami and gave the Instagram handle as @wearetazsangels_. — [XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/meet-lovely-ladies-form-tazs-angles/)
- **REPORTED (credible alt-weekly, but on a side issue):** Miami New Times described the group as a mysterious "girl gang" modeled on Charlie's Angels. It said it was unclear how the Angels made money apart from a weekly hosting gig at LIV nightclub, and that the leader's background was mysterious. It also reported that Michael "Taz" Williams' company is credited as a producer on Khia's 2002 "My Neck, My Back." - The allegations rest on three things: insinuation about the group's unexplained income, anonymous quotes from ex-"prospects", and members' photos turning up on escort sites. - Later coverage repeats the claims with less hedging over time, for example Distractify's flat "escort service." A dramatization should treat "escort service" as an allegation, never as established fact.

### Gaps
- The original Rolling Out and Don Diva articles could not be retrieved, so their actual sourcing is unknown.
- No named, on-record former member alleging sex work or coercion was found.

## 2. The reported raid on the Miami house (~early 2016) and the FBI "investigation"

### Takeaway
There is no credible confirmation of a raid, arrest or FBI investigation. Every version traces back to (a) a Don Diva claim, as relayed by BlackSportsOnline on January 29, 2016, that the FBI "believes" the group is involved in trafficking, and (b) a Hollywood Street King post on February 2, 2016 claiming a raid and arrest. HSK cited only anonymous "sources." No Miami-Dade police, FBI, DOJ, Miami Herald or local TV coverage was found. The raid story should be treated as **unsubstantiated and likely false or exaggerated gossip**. The "underage girls" element in HSK's headline is entirely uncorroborated and must not be presented as fact.

### Cited Findings
- **REPORTED (gossip blog; anonymous sourcing):** Hollywood Street King's headline reads "Taz's Angels' Home Raided, Taz Faces Human Trafficking (Underage Girls) Charges!" (dated around February 2016). It claims Taz's Miami "House of Angels" was raided "last week" in a sting and that Taz, "real name Michael J Williams," was arrested and "charged with human trafficking and tax evasion." It attributes this to unnamed "sources" and gives no case number, agency statement or court filing. A mirror exists on diaryofahollywoodstreetking.com. — [Hollywood Street King](https://hollywoodstreetking.com/tazs-angels-home-raided-human-trafficking-underage-girls-charges/); [mirror](https://diaryofahollywoodstreetking.com/tazs-angels-home-raided-human-trafficking-underage-girls-charges/)
- **REPORTED (sports/gossip blog, relaying Don Diva):** BlackSportsOnline, January 29, 2016: "Taz's Angels Being Investigated By FBI for Prostitution." Per Don Diva's online magazine, local police had long been interested but lacked a solid case, and the FBI in Miami "believe[s]" the group is part of human trafficking. It also notes "they could never get anyone to talk." — [BlackSportsOnline](https://blacksportsonline.com/2016/01/report-tazs-angels-being-investigated-by-fbi-for-escorting-photos/)
- **RUMOR (gossip blog):** Rhymes With Snitch, February 2016, "Taz Angels Under Investigation," describes it as "rumors swirling down in Miami." — [Rhymes With Snitch](https://www.rhymeswithsnitch.com/2016/02/taz-angels-under-investigation.html)
- **REPORTED (low-reliability SEO blog):** Inckredible says the group "came under federal investigation" in 2016 over trafficking allegations, but that details were "not widely publicized." It gives no source. — [Inckredible](https://inckredible.com/what-happened-to-taz-angels/)
- **Negative finding:** A targeted search for "Taz's Angels" alongside the Miami Herald, WSVN, Local10 or NBC6 returned no coverage. — [search returned only generic station pages, e.g. Local10](https://www.local10.com/topic/local_10)
- **Negative finding:** Miami New Times coverage of the group, the most credible local outlet found, mentions no raid, according to search summaries. — [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/former-tazs-angels-model-whose-iphone-was-hacked-sues-to-find-culprit-6394745/)
- The viraltalky.com "What happened to Taz Angels" page named in the brief could not be fetched (network block), and no snippet of it came up in searches. — [viraltalky](https://viraltalky.com/what-happened-to-taz-angels/)

### Inferences
- A raid on a house of Instagram-famous women in Miami, with a human-trafficking arrest involving minors, is exactly the kind of story the Miami Herald, local TV and the DOJ/USAO-SDFL press office would cover. The total absence of such coverage is strong circumstantial evidence that no such raid or federal charge happened, at least not as described.
- The chronology suggests a rumor cascade. Don Diva reported an "FBI believes" claim, BSO repeated it on January 29, Rhymes With Snitch called it "rumors" on February 1, and HSK escalated it to "raided and charged," with "underage girls" added, on February 2. Each retelling became more sensational without new sourcing.
- HSK is a gossip site, not a satire site, but the post has the markers of fabricated or unverified content: anonymous "sources," no agency named, no case number, and a sensational headline.

### Gaps
- PACER (S.D. Fla. federal docket) and the Miami-Dade Clerk could not be searched directly. A records search is needed to rule out charges definitively.
- Don Diva's original article was not located.

## 4. Public responses and lawsuits

### Takeaway
The group's response came through social media, not formal statements. Members defended a polyamorous "sister wives" arrangement and denied escorting. No interview in which Taz directly rebuts the trafficking claims was found. The one documented lawsuit tied to the group is a former member's suit over a hacked phone. No defamation suit by Taz or the group against the blogs was found.

### Cited Findings
- **REPORTED:** The Angels used Instagram to explain their relationship with Taz as "sister-wives" and to "slam critics." — [EURweb](https://eurweb.com/taz-angels-slam-critics-sister-wives-lifestyle/)
- **REPORTED:** Demi Rose denied being an escort, said the group was "not an escort agency," and said she was considering legal action over her photos being used on escort sites. No evidence that she filed suit was found. — [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/tygas-cannes-girl-former-tazs-angel-demi-rose-denies-she-hooker-1563735); [YourTango](https://www.yourtango.com/node/329773)
- **VERIFIED (alt-weekly plus legal trade press reporting on a filed lawsuit):** Former Taz's Angels model Iesha Crespo filed a lawsuit on January 21 (year not shown in the snippets; probably mid-2010s). It names "John Doe" and alleges computer fraud, emotional distress and invasion of privacy after her iPhone was hacked. Nude photos and personal information were allegedly posted, and her Instagram was renamed "Exposing Iesha Marie." The suit seeks more than $1M, and attorney Darren Heitner planned to subpoena Facebook/Instagram to identify the hacker. Suspects floated were an ex-boyfriend and a "rabid Trey Songz fan." Her mother said Crespo was with the group only from 2011 to 2012. This is not a suit against Taz or the group. — [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/former-tazs-angels-model-whose-iphone-was-hacked-sues-to-find-culprit-6394745/); [Courthouse News](https://courthousenews.com/florida-modelsphone-hacked/)
- **Negative finding:** Searches found no on-record interview of Taz addressing the prostitution rumors. — [search context: BlackSportsOnline](https://blacksportsonline.com/2016/01/report-tazs-angels-being-investigated-by-fbi-for-escorting-photos/)

### Inferences
- Because Taz never sued over the 2016 blog claims, the claims were never adjudicated as false. That does not make them true: suing gossip blogs is often impractical.
- The Crespo suit is a usable, documented plot point (cyber-harassment and leaked images), but it was against an unknown hacker, not the group.

### Gaps
- The outcome of the Crespo suit, and its exact filing year, are unknown.
- It is unknown whether any former member ever sued Taz, for example over wages, contracts or likeness.

## 5. Decline: when and why activity stopped; rebrands

### Takeaway
The group's peak visibility ran from about 2012 to 2015. Prominent members left (Demi Rose in November 2015), and the 2016 FBI/raid rumors hit soon after. Afterward the brand fragmented into "Taz's Angels Uncensored" (TikTok/Snapchat content), a music pivot, and Taz's "PolyFly" polyamory-coaching business. Credible accounts of why it declined are lacking. Explainers attribute it to the investigation rumors, waning public interest and member departures.

### Cited Findings
- **REPORTED (Wikipedia, for date):** Demi Rose left in November 2015. — [Wikipedia: Demi Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demi_Rose)
- **REPORTED (low-reliability blog):** "As the investigation continued and public interest waned, Taz's Angels began to disperse." Taz "kept a lower profile following the controversy." — [Inckredible](https://inckredible.com/what-happened-to-taz-angels/)
- **REPORTED (fan site):** The group "reinvented themselves as Taz's Angels Uncensored," with many new faces. A past-members page exists. — [lovetazsangel.wixsite.com: Uncensored](https://lovetazsangel.wixsite.com/tazsangels/taz-s-angels-uncensored); [Past Members](https://lovetazsangel.wixsite.com/tazsangels/pastmembers)
- **Primary (self-published account):** The TikTok account @tazsangelsuncensored exists, tagline "The Future of Entertainment -Taz." — [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@tazsangelsuncensored)
- **RUMOR (forum/YouTube description):** "Failed attempts to rebrand as Taz's Angels Uncensored and a pivot to music failed to recapture their previous status." — [Boxden](https://boxden.com/showthread.php?t=3552962)
- **Primary (self-published):** The Taz's Angels Snapchat (@tazsangelstv) and X (@TazsAngels) accounts still exist, with a Palm Beach, FL location. The Snapchat showed activity in 2025. — [Snapchat](https://www.snapchat.com/@tazsangelstv?locale=en-US); [X @TazsAngels](https://x.com/TazsAngels)
- **Note:** The original Instagram handle cited by XXL was @wearetazsangels_. Its current status (active, deleted or banned) could not be confirmed. — [XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/meet-lovely-ladies-form-tazs-angles/)

### Inferences
- This looks less like a sudden collapse than a slow fade and a pivot: the brand moved from Instagram nightlife to Snapchat/TikTok "uncensored" content and then to coaching.

### Gaps
- No credible dated account exists of when the Miami house closed or when the main Instagram stopped posting. The claim of a move to Palm Beach rests only on account location tags.

## Who were the most prominent/public members, who were the "originals," and did any become famous independently?

### Takeaway
The group was founded around 2011 by Michael "Taz" Williams. Its best-known "core six" were Cat Da Great, AB, Kinky, Ashley, Ruby and Leena; Ruby and Leena are sisters. The group was at one point described as up to eight women, and it shrank to two: Cat and AB, who are still with Taz as self-described "sister wives" in 2026. The best-documented independent successes are Iesha Marie (Iesha Crespo), who appeared in Drake videos and HBO's *Ballers*, and Leena Sayed, who later became the publicly known partner of boxer Devin Haney.

## How did the hierarchy work (Taz, Cat, senior Angels, "prospects")? Rules, curfews, dress codes, finances, phone and social control, living arrangements?

### Takeaway
Publicly, the structure was one man (Taz) at the top, with Cat as the lead woman. Full "Angels" ranked above "prospects," the women trying out for a spot, and the members lived together in a South Beach mansion. The group's own framing is a polygynous "sister wives" family with "12 principles," now sold as a paid "PolyFly" membership. Specific house rules (sleeping arrangements, no phone, money) come only from a former prospect's account, which survives through gossip-site reposts. I found no documented curfew or dress code.

### Cited Findings
**Living arrangements and money**
- VERIFIED: Miami New Times (2014), citing the group's videos, reported that Taz housed five women in a $4.5 million South Beach mansion near Alton Road. It said it was unclear how the Angels made money apart from a weekly hosting gig at LIV nightclub. — [Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/former-tazs-angels-model-whose-iphone-was-hacked-sues-to-find-culprit-6394745/)
- REPORTED (self-description): Coverage describes the group as women living in one home, all devoted to one man. - The "12 principles" are the closest thing to a published rulebook. Their content wasn't visible in search results, and the PolyFly site would be the primary source for a writer.

The most serious insider claims come from an anonymous former prospect around 2016, reported through gossip sites: "I was literally a sex slave in the house" and "there's something really off about Taz," and she called him "very very mean."

# Separating Fact From Rumor in Taz's Angels

Taz's Angels has two histories, and they barely overlap. The documented one is small. Michael "Taz" Williams, a Tampa-area music executive who produced Khia's 2001-02 hit "My Neck, My Back," built an Instagram-famous Miami "girl gang" modeled on *Charlie's Angels*. It peaked between roughly 2013 and 2015 with a weekly hosting gig at LIV nightclub, a South Beach mansion shown in its own videos, and an account that passed one million followers in September 2015. The rumored history is far larger: an escort ring for celebrities, a cult-like "prospect" system, an FBI investigation, and a 2016 raid with trafficking charges involving minors. Every piece of that second story comes from gossip blogs, forum posts, or anonymous former "prospects." No police record, court filing, or mainstream report of a raid, arrest, or charge was found. Only two items are court-grade or credibly reported: a former member's 2014 lawsuit against an unknown hacker, and the December 2024 leak of a jail call between Young Thug and ex-member Leena Sayed. The group never collapsed in a single event. It faded through departures and rebrands into "The PolyFly Experience," a paid polygyny curriculum that Taz runs with his two remaining partners, Cat and AB, who were still promoting it in November 2026. Drake's May 2026 lyric "what happened to Taz's Angels?" put the group back in the news. For a dramatized series, the safe material is the documented spine. Everything darker has to be presented as allegation or fictionalized.

## How to read this report

Every claim carries one of three tags. **[VERIFIED]** means a credible outlet, court filing, on-record statement, or primary post. When the source is the group's own channel, the tag only confirms that the group *said* it. **[REPORTED]** means a single entertainment or gossip outlet, a fan site, or an encyclopedia-style aggregator, with no primary documentation. **[RUMOR]** covers forums, anonymous accounts, tabloid blogs with unnamed "sources," AI summaries, and fan interpretation. "ALLEGATION" is added where a rumor accuses someone of wrongdoing.

**Source limitation:** the research team could not open most original articles. Fetches to Miami New Times, XXL, Bossip, EURweb, Sportskeeda, Hollywood Street King, and others were blocked in the research environment, so almost every finding comes from search-result snippets and summaries. Quotes may be truncated, and several dates were inferred from URL paths or decoded post IDs. **Check every quote, date, and characterization against the original article before it goes into a script, pitch, or marketing copy.** No court docket (PACER, Miami-Dade or Palm Beach clerks) or Florida corporate filing was searched directly.

**Privacy handling:** people who publicly use only a stage name are referred to by that name (Cat, Cat Da Great, AB, Kinky, Ashley, Demi Rose). No home or family addresses are included. One source printed the street address of a family residence. It has been deliberately omitted.

## A Khia producer turned a Tampa label résumé into Miami's "it" girls

Before the Angels, Taz worked in music. He is credited as producer, with the Plat'num House team, on Khia's "My Neck, My Back (Lick It)." He was also executive producer of her album *Thug Misses*, which came out on Divine Records on October 30, 2001 and was re-released nationally on April 23, 2002 through his Dirty Down Records with Artemis **[VERIFIED]** ([Wikipedia: Thug Misses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thug_Misses); [WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/Khia/My-Neck,-My-Back-(Lick-It)/)). The single peaked at **No. 42 on the Hot 100** **[VERIFIED]** ([Wikipedia: Khia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Khia)). The relationship ended badly. After leaving Dirty Down, Khia told MTV that her old label was "the ones that's been spreading the rumors." That is her side only, and no reply from the label was found **[VERIFIED, one-sided]** ([MTV News](https://www.mtv.com/news/1455425/khia-shocked-by-response-to-my-neck-my-back/)). The producer origin is still little known. One X user was surprised to learn it from a YouTube documentary as late as February 2026 ([X](https://x.com/downtown7thave/status/2025459812570440056)).

Miami New Times reported that his entertainment company, **Gigolo Enterprises LLC**, was registered in Lutz, a Tampa suburb **[VERIFIED]**. The paper also said that "their leader's background is mysterious" ([Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/former-tazs-angels-model-whose-iphone-was-hacked-sues-to-find-culprit-6394745/); [Miami New Times, second article](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/a-former-tazs-angels-model-has-photo-hacked-sues-to-find-culprit-6560610/)). No source gives his age or birth year, and his activities between 2002 and the group's launch are undocumented. A tabloid claims he styled himself "The Black Hugh Hefner" **[RUMOR]** ([Hollywood Street King](https://hollywoodstreetking.com/tazs-angels-prostitution-sex-trafficking-ring/)). Cat and AB still call him **"The Chosen One"** in a recorded Instagram Live **[REPORTED, self-description]** ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VPGqigYLqQQ)). His own PolyFly website calls him an "Entrepreneur, founder of Dirty Down Records, founder of Taz's Angels, and creator of PolyFly" **[VERIFIED as his claim]** ([The PolyFly Experience](https://www.thepolyflyexperience.com/)).

Secondary sources usually give **2011** as the founding year **[REPORTED]** ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/pop-culture/what-taz-s-angels-members-explored-amid-devin-haney-s-partner-leena-sayed-young-thug-jail-call-controversy)). There is evidence the group existed earlier. When Kinky left in January 2016 she cited "eight years of devoted partying" **[REPORTED]** ([HelloBeautiful](https://hellobeautiful.com/2837964/draya-comes-for-taz-angels/)), and the official @TazsAngels X account was already posting, signed "~Cat," by October 2012 **[VERIFIED]** ([X](https://x.com/tazsangels/status/252866657410752512?lang=en)). The name combines Taz with *Charlie's Angels*. Miami New Times called the group "a mysterious girl gang modeled after Charlie's Angels" **[VERIFIED]** ([Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/former-tazs-angels-model-whose-iphone-was-hacked-sues-to-find-culprit-6394745/)). The earliest dated web traces are from 2013. That year IMDb lists a video titled "Taz's Angels: Kinky and Anabella BTS and Bday Celebration" ([IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt8857144/)), and on December 3, 2013 a blog featured the Instagram handle @tazsangels_ ([IrresponsibleLife](https://irresponsiblelife.com/2013/12/03/irresponsible-instagram-tazsangels_/)), both **[REPORTED]**. XXL gives the handle as @wearetazsangels_ and called the women the "it" girls of Miami **[VERIFIED that the feature exists; contents unread]** ([XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/meet-lovely-ladies-form-tazs-angles/)). Audience figures grew from **"almost 500,000" Instagram followers** in Miami New Times's mid-2010s coverage to an account that **"first reached 1 million followers in September of 2015"** **[REPORTED]** ([Famous Birthdays](https://www.famousbirthdays.com/webgroups/tazs-angels.html)).

Cat (Cat Da Great, @iamcatdagreat) is the second lead. She is consistently described as the alpha female, house mother, recruiter, and public voice. Critics call her the "madame" **[REPORTED / ALLEGATION for "madame"]** ([Lipstick Alley repost of Rolling Out](https://www.lipstickalley.com/threads/5-things-to-know-about-taz%C2%92s-angels-pimp-escorts-and-sex-trafficking-rolling-out-magazine.1160929/)). Accounts of how she was recruited conflict, as covered in the conflicts section below. Taz stays mostly off-mic in the record, while Cat speaks for the group in every documented feud and defense. That gives a drama its natural two-hander.

## Club hosting is the only income anyone ever confirmed

The most credible outlet to examine the group answered the money question with a shrug. Miami New Times wrote: "**It's unclear how the Angels make money, apart from a weekly hosting gig at LIV nightclub**" at the Fontainebleau **[VERIFIED]** ([Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/a-former-tazs-angels-model-has-photo-hacked-sues-to-find-culprit-6560610/)). Several other ventures are reported but not documented in detail. The group hosted "Concept" trips at nightclubs in other U.S. cities ([Boxden summary](https://boxden.com/showthread.php?t=3567358)). It ran a lifestyle and events banner called **"Bad B*tches Link Up"** (BBLU) and an apparel line, **Caviar Blaque** ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/pop-culture/what-taz-s-angels-members-explored-amid-devin-haney-s-partner-leena-sayed-young-thug-jail-call-controversy); [Prezi: Caviar Blaque](https://prezi.com/e5rcydmiljpy/caviar-blaque-by-tazs-angels/)). All of these are **[REPORTED]**. Forum posters later claimed the group sold a premium Snapchat for about $20 **[RUMOR]** ([Lipstick Alley](https://www.lipstickalley.com/threads/tazs-angels-struggling-comeback-discussion-thread-lets-keep-this-clean.1198766/)). No source documents a hosting fee, appearance rate, sponsorship, or how money was split between Taz and the women. That gap is the central ambiguity the rumors filled.

The public image was the mansion. According to Miami New Times, Taz "puts a group of five women up in a **$4.5 million South Beach mansion**, according to their videos, and instructs them to take endless photos of one another" **[VERIFIED, based on the group's own videos]** ([Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/a-former-tazs-angels-model-has-photo-hacked-sues-to-find-culprit-6560610/)). A Rolling Out piece, seen only as a forum repost, says Cat explained the group's choice to "link up and sleep in empty mansions." That suggests the houses may have been rented, borrowed, or staged **[REPORTED]** ([Lipstick Alley repost](https://www.lipstickalley.com/threads/5-things-to-know-about-taz%C2%92s-angels-pimp-escorts-and-sex-trafficking-rolling-out-magazine.1160929/)). The aesthetic was Playboy Mansion, and the group posted a tribute when Hugh Hefner died in September 2017 **[REPORTED]** ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/pop-culture/what-taz-s-angels-members-explored-amid-devin-haney-s-partner-leena-sayed-young-thug-jail-call-controversy)).

The household had a visible hierarchy. Taz was at the top, with Cat as senior woman, then the full Angels, then **"prospects."** A fan site defines a prospect as "a woman who is in the running for a spot in the house with the Angels… considered a member in training" and keeps separate prospect galleries **[REPORTED]** ([fan site: Prospects](https://lovetazsangel.wixsite.com/tazsangels/copy-of-past-members)). Cat also polled followers on whether to add a new Angel ([Rolling Out via Lipstick Alley](https://www.lipstickalley.com/threads/5-things-to-know-about-taz%C2%92s-angels-pimp-escorts-and-sex-trafficking-rolling-out-magazine.1160929/)).

What happened inside the house comes only from anonymous sources. A November 2014 blog account says recruitment started with Instagram likes and heart-eye DMs. It says guests were then pressured to drink at the mansion and told to wait for Taz, and that sex with him was the initiation test **[RUMOR / ALLEGATION]** ([TheChatRoom](https://www.thechatroom.tv/post/2014/11/03/exclusive-what-do-the-taz-angels-really-do)). A self-described recent prospect posted on a forum that "that house is something crazy." The poster said Cat lectured prospects on how privileged they were "to see the other side of the doors" **[RUMOR]** ([TheColi](https://www.thecoli.com/threads/i-was-one-of-taz-angels-prospects-in-the-last-recent-months-and-that-house-is-something-crazy.382187/)). A former prospect interviewed by Fameolous, an interview the researchers could not locate and that survives only through reposts, claimed three things: prospects "were all forced to share a bed," "you had to do things for money to eat or shop," and she had no phone or car **[RUMOR / ALLEGATION]** ([Lipstick Alley repost of Rolling Out](https://www.lipstickalley.com/threads/5-things-to-know-about-taz%C2%92s-angels-pimp-escorts-and-sex-trafficking-rolling-out-magazine.1160929/)). No contract, NDA, curfew, dress code, or written rulebook has ever surfaced. The defense addresses consent and polygyny. It never addresses money or coercion directly, and that gap between the public story and the insider allegations is the core dramatic tension the sources support.

## The 2016 raid story collapses under scrutiny

The escort allegations grew from gossip blogs between 2014 and 2016. The Chat Room asked "What Do The Taz Angels Really Do?" in November 2014 ([TheChatRoom](https://www.thechatroom.tv/post/2014/11/03/exclusive-what-do-the-taz-angels-really-do)). A French-Caribbean blog asked whether the group was "un phénomène de prostitution" (a prostitution phenomenon) in March 2015 ([L'Antillais](https://lantillaisblog.wordpress.com/2015/03/15/tazs-angels-un-phenomene-de-prostitution/)). Baltimore's 92Q ran "PROSTITUTION exposed" ([92Q](https://92q.com/3883949/prostitution-exposed-those-taz-angels-aint-who-they-post-to-be/)). Even The Shade Room conceded the women "may not technically (as in it hasn't been proven) be prostitutes" ([The Shade Room](https://theshaderoom.com/tazs-angels-give-a-quick-glimpse-into-how-they-operate/)). All of these are **[RUMOR / ALLEGATION]**. Some "evidence" was really image theft. In 2016 Demi Rose said her Instagram photos had been copied onto escort sites worldwide, that this happened to "many girls in the business," and that she was weighing legal action **[REPORTED]** ([IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/tygas-cannes-girl-former-tazs-angel-demi-rose-denies-she-hooker-1563735)). Later coverage repeats the claims with less hedging. Distractify flatly calls the group a "now-inactive Snapchat-based escort service" without sourcing ([Distractify](https://www.distractify.com/p/demi-rose-dating-history)).

The raid story can be traced through four posts in five days. On **January 29, 2016**, BlackSportsOnline relayed a Don Diva claim that the FBI in Miami "believe[s]" the group is involved in human trafficking, while noting that police "could never get anyone to talk" **[REPORTED claim of a claim]** ([BlackSportsOnline](https://blacksportsonline.com/2016/01/report-tazs-angels-being-investigated-by-fbi-for-escorting-photos/)). Rhymes With Snitch then described "rumors swirling down in Miami" **[RUMOR]** ([Rhymes With Snitch](https://www.rhymeswithsnitch.com/2016/02/taz-angels-under-investigation.html)). Around **February 2**, Hollywood Street King escalated to "**Taz's Angels' Home Raided, Taz Faces Human Trafficking (Underage Girls) Charges!**" It claimed a sting raid "last week" and charges of human trafficking and tax evasion, sourced only to unnamed "sources." It gave no agency, case number, or filing **[RUMOR / ALLEGATION]** ([Hollywood Street King](https://hollywoodstreetking.com/tazs-angels-home-raided-human-trafficking-underage-girls-charges/)). The Don Diva original was never located, and the "underage" element appears in no earlier source.

A raid, an arrest, and a trafficking case involving minors at the home of Instagram-famous women in Miami would normally be covered by the Miami Herald, local TV, and the U.S. Attorney's Office press office. The researchers searched those outlets and found **no coverage at all** ([search returned only generic pages, e.g., Local10](https://www.local10.com/topic/local_10)). Miami New Times, the most credible local outlet on the group, mentions no raid ([Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/former-tazs-angels-model-whose-iphone-was-hacked-sues-to-find-culprit-6394745/)). Taz's continued public activity after 2016 also cuts against an arrest: IMDb credits in 2022 and 2025, music releases in 2024 and 2025, and PolyFly. A forum poster summed up the folk consensus as "taz never got locked up" **[RUMOR]** ([Boxden](https://boxden.com/showthread.php?t=3552962)). The conclusion: **no verifiable arrest, charge, indictment, or conviction of Michael "Taz" Williams exists in any source reviewed, and the raid story reads as an escalating rumor chain.** Two caveats apply. A direct PACER and county-clerk search was not possible. And "Michael Williams" is a very common name. ## For the series writer: where the legal risk sits

*This section is practical guidance, not legal advice. Have an entertainment or media lawyer review scripts before production or release.*

The legal exposure in this story is unusually lopsided. Nearly all of the commercially appealing material (escorting, pimping, a raid, trafficking, minors, celebrity clients) is the material that has never been substantiated. The people involved are real, identifiable, and in several cases still public figures. Defamation and false-light claims arise when a dramatization implies false statements of fact about identifiable people. Florida, where most of the cast lives, also has a statutory right-of-publicity law covering commercial use of a person's name or likeness without consent. Expressive works get some room, but an AI-generated series that recreates real faces or voices raises the stakes considerably. These are the main pressure points:

| Risk area | What to avoid | Safer approach |
|---|---|---|
| Arrests, raids, charges | Showing Taz or anyone else being raided, arrested, charged, or convicted as historical fact. No record supports it | Fictional characters, or frame clearly as a rumor characters hear and react to |
| Minors | Any depiction or implication that minors were involved. The "underage" claim appears only in one anonymously sourced blog headline | Leave it out entirely |
| Escort and sex-work claims | Presenting escorting, pimping, or trafficking as what the group "really" did | Keep it as an unresolved allegation inside the story, ideally on fictionalized characters |
| Named ex-members | Tying **Iesha Crespo, Leena Sayed, or Demi Rose** to sex work. Demi Rose publicly denied it, and nothing links the others to it | Don't use their names or recognizable likenesses for any sex-work storyline. Composite or invented Angels are safer |
| Anonymous insider claims | Turning the "sex slave," shared-bed, and no-phone claims into scenes attributed to a real, named person | Assign them to fictional prospects; make the source's unreliability part of the drama if useful |
| Celebrities | Depicting Drake, Draya, Young Thug, Haney, Irv Gotti, or Lil Wayne as clients or participants in anything beyond the reported public moments | Use fictional celebrities, or stay within the reported public record (the Cameo argument, the public feud, the leaked call), framed as reported |
| Stage-name people | Revealing legal names of people who publicly use only stage names (Cat, AB, Kinky, others) | Use invented names |
| AI likeness and voice | Generating the real faces or voices of any of these people, or reproducing Drake's lyric without a license | Original character designs and voices; license or paraphrase lyrics |

The clearest path is to **fictionalize names and identifying details and bill the series as "inspired by" the 2010s Miami Instagram-crew era**. Pair that with an on-screen disclaimer that characters and events are fictionalized and that real-world allegations against any real person were never proven. That keeps the dramatically rich parts available: the charismatic ex-producer, the loyal senior partner, the prospect system, the club rivalries, the rumor storm, the members who got out and found fame. It also removes the claim that a real person did something they may not have done. If the series keeps real names anywhere, keep those scenes to the VERIFIED column of this report and run the specific scripts past counsel.

## Conclusion

The striking finding is how little of the Taz's Angels legend survives checking. The darkest version of the story, a raid and trafficking charges involving minors, came from four posts over five days in early 2016, each more sensational than the last and none adding a source. A decade of explainers has since repeated it until it reads like settled history. The documented story is less lurid but arguably better drama. It runs from a forgotten producer's second act through Instagram celebrity before influencer houses existed, then a public relationship that critics called exploitation and the participants called family, and ends with the two loyalists who stayed now selling that arrangement as a curriculum. The open question the record leaves, where the money came from and what the women gave up, is exactly the space fiction can explore, provided the characters asking it are invented. Before any of this is locked into a series bible, the most valuable research steps are reading the full Miami New Times articles to fix the dates, pulling the two YouTube transcripts, and running a PACER and Florida clerk search to close the arrest question for good.
